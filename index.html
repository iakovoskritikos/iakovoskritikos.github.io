Identity & Research Focus

An architect of adaptive virtual-reality systems for medicine — treatment environments that read a person's physiological signals in real time and change what they deliver in response.

My work centers on closed-loop, physiologically adaptive virtual reality for psychiatric and neurological care, combining real-time electrophysiological signal processing (electrodermal activity, EEG, motion tracking), human-computer interaction, and high-throughput research informatics.

Independent Academic Benchmarking: In 2026, an independent research group at the University of Alberta rebuilt this closed-loop adaptive VR methodology as the benchmark for their own system (ACM Trans. Interactive Intelligent Systems 16(1)), describing it as, to the best of their knowledge, the only prior adaptive-VR work for arachnophobia — establishing it as the reference point the field measures against.
